# Visual Forms

Distil first, then pick the form, then write only what the form cannot carry. The order is fixed:

```
CORE POINT → RELATIONSHIP → FORM → FEWEST WORDS → DETAIL BEHIND A DISCLOSURE
```

## Core point first

Before writing a section, answer: what is the one point, how many key points hold it up, how they relate, which form shows that relation fastest, and which details the reader truly needs.

- One heading, one point. One box or node, one idea.
- A heading states the point ("Licenses earn, hardware shrinks"), not the topic ("Segments").
- Words carry what the form cannot: the why, the condition, the exception.
- Cut lead-ins, restatements and sentences that add no understanding. Keep every figure, condition, risk and exception: cutting moves detail down a level, never off the page.
- Length follows the topic's complexity, never a quota of words or bullets.
- When a visual carries a fact, drop the sentence beside it that repeats it.

## Pick the form by relationship

| Relationship in the content | Form | Fits | Use another form when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Category and sub-category, each item under one parent | **Tree**, each branch named with a short line on what it does | segments to revenue streams, product lines, customer groups | an item sits under two parents: use a matrix or cards |
| Layers, each resting on the one below | **Stack**; a **pyramid** only when the layers really narrow in count or rank, and the evidence says so | technology stack, infrastructure up to the end service | the layers are a plain list, or the shape would imply a ranking the evidence lacks |
| Order, with branches where a condition changes the path | **Flow** | how the product works, chip design, how revenue is made, a decision | the steps have no real order |
| Parts that exchange something | **System diagram**, every arrow labelled with what moves (data, money, goods, dependency) | ecosystem, value chain, supplier, company and customer | value moves one way only: use a flow |
| Change over time | **Timeline** | company history, roadmap, catalysts for the thesis | the dates only decorate |
| Options on shared dimensions | **Matrix** | peers, products, business models, Bull against Bear, scenarios | the dimensions do not bear on the decision |
| A state that changes, and the cause | **Animation**: show what changes and why, readable at rest, honouring reduced motion | a product's mechanism, flow of data or energy, cause and effect, a problem and its fix | a still picture already shows it, or the outcome has not happened yet |
| Short items with no structure between them | **Cards or bullets** | three strengths, key risks, KPIs, open questions | (the default; no diagram needed) |

A page uses only the forms its content needs; a question gets one form.

## Disclose in levels

1. **Core point**: what the reader must take away.
2. **Visual explanation**: the form that shows how the points relate.
3. **Supporting evidence**: figures, sources, assumptions.
4. **Deep dive**: technical detail and full tables.

A reader understands the whole from Levels 1 and 2; every detail of Levels 3 and 4 stays one tap away.

## Accuracy over polish

- A simpler picture that changes the meaning is wrong: keep the real structure, or use words.
- Draw a causal arrow only where evidence supports the cause, and label a node FACT, INFERENCE or UNKNOWN as you would the sentence.
- Where data is missing, put UNKNOWN in its slot and name what is missing in one line.
- When no form shows it correctly, write it as text.
