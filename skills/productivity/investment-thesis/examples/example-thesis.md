# Example thesis: Northfield Grid Systems (hypothetical)

> **Illustration only.** Northfield is a fictional company and every number below is invented to show the *reasoning*, not to describe any real company or market. It is not a recommendation. A real thesis cites a dated source for every FACT.

Condensed to the chain links that show the method; a full thesis fills all 20 sections of `templates/thesis-template.md`.

## Investment question

How structural is Northfield's advantage in large power transformers, and how much of the value created by grid investment for AI data centers can it capture over the next 7 years?

## Megatrend

```
AI adoption → compute demand → data center build-out → electricity demand
→ grid interconnection and upgrades → large power transformer demand
```

- Why now: data center power requests outpace the grid's planned additions (FACT, would cite utility interconnection queue data).
- Where value flows: to the equipment stage where capacity is slowest to add (INFERENCE, from lead-time evidence below).

## Industry

- Importance: **Critical**. No practical substitute for transformers in grid connection; outages cascade to every downstream user; governments list them as strategic (FACT, would cite government grid reports).
- Structure: top 5 makers hold about 60% of capacity in the target region (FACT, would cite industry association data; share measured by capacity, regional). Customers are utilities and data center developers, fragmented across many buyers. Utility qualification takes 2 to 4 years per design. Verdict: structure supports sustained profits for qualified incumbents (INFERENCE).

## Bottleneck

- Lead times rose from about 12 months to about 36 months in four years; makers allocate slots and take deposits (FACT, would cite industry surveys and filings).
- Constraint types: capacity, qualification, specialty steel supply.
- Northfield exposure: 55% of revenue and 70% of operating profit from large power transformers (FACT, would cite segment reporting).
- Pricing: new orders priced 30% above orders from three years ago (FACT, earnings call; Tier 2).
- Capture check: margin uplift is visible but partly offset by fixed-price legacy contracts still in backlog (INFERENCE).

## Company position and advantage

- Position: inside the bottleneck stage, with qualified designs at most large regional utilities.
- Core advantage: **qualification barrier plus installed base**. Why: utilities requalify slowly and standardise on approved designs. Evidence: win rate on tenders where it is pre-qualified is roughly double the rate where it is not (FACT, investor day). Replication: a new entrant needs a factory (3 years) plus qualification (2 to 4 years). Durability: **Medium** (3 to 7 years), because two competitors have announced new factories.

## Economic capture

```
Grid spend ↑ → transformer spend ↑ → Northfield revenue ↑ → price realisation ↑
→ gross margin 22% → 29% → FCF conversion 85% → ROIC 9% → 17%
```

- Leakage: capex for its own expansion absorbs about half of the incremental FCF (FACT, capex guidance); specialty steel suppliers raised prices 15% (FACT).
- Verdict: captures a meaningful share of the scarcity today; the share depends on pricing discipline once rival capacity arrives (INFERENCE).

## Financial evidence

- Supports: margin expansion coincides with price realisation, not volume alone; incremental ROIC above cost of capital (FACT).
- Contradicts: margins were 12% in the previous downcycle, so part of today's margin is cyclical scarcity, not moat (INFERENCE).
- Verdict: **mixed**, decided by the downcycle margin and the incremental ROIC.

## Second-order effects

- Lead times ↑ → utilities sign multi-year framework agreements → revenue visibility ↑ (helps; FACT, two such agreements disclosed).
- Prices ↑ → rivals and new entrants add capacity → pricing ↓ from year 3 (hurts; INFERENCE from announced factories).
- Frontier: whether utilities redesign grids to need fewer large units is unevidenced, so the chain stops here.

## Counter-thesis

AI efficiency gains plus on-site generation at data centers slow grid connection demand, while announced capacity from three rivals lands in the same 2-year window, turning today's shortage into a capital-cycle glut. Probability: **Medium** (evidence: announced capacity totals about 40% of current regional output).

## Thesis breakers

| Breaker | Indicator | Threshold | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bottleneck relieved | Industry lead time | Below 18 months | Industry association survey |
| Pricing power gone | Price on new orders versus backlog | New orders priced below backlog for 2 quarters | Earnings calls |
| Moat erodes | Northfield tender win rate where pre-qualified | Falls toward the non-qualified rate | Investor day, filings |
| Economics deteriorate | ROIC versus WACC | Below WACC for a full year | Annual report |

## What must be true

For a valuation that implies 10% revenue growth for 7 years and a 27% steady-state gross margin: lead times stay above 24 months through year 4, Northfield holds share as rival capacity arrives, and legacy fixed-price contracts roll off on schedule.
