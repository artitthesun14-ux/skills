# Investment framework

How to start a thesis: frame the question, trace the megatrend to money, and keep asking why.

## The investment question

A good question names three things:

- **The company** and the business line under study.
- **The industry** whose growth it hopes to capture.
- **What winning means**: share gain, pricing power, margin expansion, returns above cost of capital, over a stated horizon.

Template: "How structural is **[company]**'s advantage in **[industry]**, and how much of the economic value created by **[industry]**'s growth can it capture over **[horizon]**?"

Ask the user only when the choice changes the analysis materially (which segment of a conglomerate, which listing, a 2-year versus 10-year horizon). Otherwise pick the most defensible framing and state it.

## Megatrend to money

A megatrend matters only through the chain of demand it creates. Write it as an arrow chain, one mechanism per arrow:

```
AI adoption → compute demand → data center build-out → electricity demand
→ grid investment → transformer demand → supplier lead times → supplier pricing power
```

Drivers to scan: technology, AI, automation, demographics, energy, electrification, infrastructure, regulation, geopolitics, resource scarcity, consumer behaviour.

For the chain, answer four questions:

| Question | What a good answer has |
| --- | --- |
| Why now? | The specific change (cost curve, regulation, capacity gap) that makes this decade different. |
| Why does it matter? | Size of the demand shift, with a sourced number. |
| What changes? | Which spending, behaviour or capacity moves, and by how much. |
| Where does value flow? | The link where spend becomes someone's margin: hand this to the value chain step. |

The chain ends at a named pool of profit. If it ends at a trend word, keep going.

## First-principles "why" chain

Ask "why?" until the answer is an economic fact you can check:

```
AI needs power
  why? compute draws electricity
  why is that a bottleneck? grid capacity grows slower than demand
  why slower? permitting, transformer supply, transmission build times
  who captures value? equipment suppliers with constrained capacity
  which supplier has the advantage? check share, backlog, qualification, ROIC
```

The chain is finished when the last line names evidence to fetch. A first narrative that sounds right is the starting point for this chain, not its end.
