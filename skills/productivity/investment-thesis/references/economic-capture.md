# Economic capture

The core module: how much of the value the industry creates does the company keep?

## The capture chain

```
Industry growth → value created → value chain split → company position
→ pricing power → margin → free cash flow → ROIC
```

Each arrow can leak value. Name where it leaks: to customers (bargaining power), to suppliers (input costs), to rivals (capacity additions), or to capex (growth that consumes the cash it creates).

## What to measure

| Metric | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Pricing power | Price increases achieved versus input cost inflation. |
| Gross margin | Value kept after direct costs; compare to peers in the same stage. |
| Operating margin | Value kept after running the business. |
| Incremental margin | Change in operating profit divided by change in revenue: whether growth adds value. |
| FCF and FCF conversion | FCF divided by net income: whether profits become cash. |
| ROIC | Returns on capital versus cost of capital. |
| Incremental ROIC | Change in NOPAT divided by change in invested capital: whether new capital earns its keep. |
| Capital intensity | Capex as a share of revenue: how much growth costs. |
| Customer power | Concentration, contract terms, ability to dual-source or insource. |
| Supplier power | Concentration of key inputs, pass-through clauses. |

## The capture verdict

Answer: **how much economic value can the company capture from the industry?**

Express it as a share estimate where possible (e.g. "about 10% of industry spend becomes its revenue, and 20% of that becomes FCF"), label it INFERENCE or ASSUMPTION, and name the leakage that most limits it.
