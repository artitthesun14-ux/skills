# Industry analysis

Two questions, answered before the company enters the picture: how much does the world need this industry, and does its structure let winners keep their profits?

## Industry importance

Assess each dimension with evidence:

| Dimension | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Economic | Share of GDP, capex or spend it represents, or enables. |
| Infrastructure | Do other critical systems depend on it? |
| Technological | Is it a required input to a key technology? |
| Substitutability | Can buyers switch to another product or process, and at what cost and delay? |
| Supply shock | What happens downstream if supply stops for 6 months? |
| Strategic | Do governments treat it as strategic (subsidies, export controls, national champions)? |
| Regulatory | Is it licensed, mandated or protected by regulation? |

Rate **Low / Medium / High / Critical** and justify the rating with the two or three dimensions that drive it. A rating without cited evidence is an ASSUMPTION.

- **Critical**: hard to substitute, a supply stop disrupts other critical systems, governments intervene.
- **High**: important input with limited substitutes, but disruption is containable.
- **Medium**: meaningful but substitutable within a few years.
- **Low**: discretionary or easily substituted.

## Industry structure

Cover each, with figures and sources:

- Market size and growth (historical and forecast, with the forecaster named).
- Concentration: top-3 or top-5 share, HHI if available. Check how share is defined (revenue, units, capacity; global or regional).
- Competitors and their capacity plans.
- Supplier concentration and customer concentration.
- Switching costs and qualification requirements.
- Capital intensity and minimum efficient scale.
- Technology barriers and IP.
- Regulation and licensing.
- Distribution and installed base.

## The structural verdict

Answer: **does this structure let the winner sustain profits over the long run?**

Structures that tend to sustain profits: concentrated supply, fragmented customers, high switching or qualification costs, long-lived installed base, capacity that is slow to add.

Structures that tend to erode them: commodity product, concentrated and powerful customers, low barriers to capacity additions, fast technology turnover, frequent capital-cycle overbuilds.

State the verdict as an INFERENCE and list the evidence it rests on.
