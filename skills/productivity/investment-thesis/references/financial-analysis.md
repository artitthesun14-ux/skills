# Financial analysis

Test the thesis against the numbers. Every metric answers one question: **do the financials support or contradict the advantage claimed?**

## What to pull (at least 5 years, more for cyclicals)

- **Revenue**: total growth, organic growth, segment growth, recurring share.
- **Margins**: gross, EBIT, incremental.
- **Cash flow**: FCF, capex (maintenance versus growth where disclosed), FCF conversion.
- **Capital efficiency**: ROIC, incremental ROIC, versus cost of capital.
- **Balance sheet**: net debt, liquidity, interest coverage, maturities.

Cite the filing and period for each figure.

Read **exposure first**: what share of revenue and profit the thesis actually touches. When it is a small or undisclosed share, say so and read the reported segment instead, because a consolidated margin then describes a different business.

## Link each number to a claim

| If the thesis claims | The financials should show |
| --- | --- |
| Pricing power | Gross margin rising or stable while input costs rise. |
| Scale advantage | Operating margin expanding with volume; unit costs below peers. |
| Switching costs | Low churn, recurring revenue, stable share. |
| Bottleneck capture | Backlog growth, price realisation, margin above the cycle average. |
| Durable moat | ROIC above cost of capital through a full cycle. |
| Capital-light growth | High FCF conversion, incremental ROIC holding up. |

## Contradictions to report

State each one plainly. Typical ones: margins high only at the cycle peak, growth funded by debt or acquisitions, FCF far below net income, incremental ROIC falling while revenue grows, segment profits concentrated in one customer.

Close the loop with the bottleneck step: pricing power that leaves no trace in revenue mix, margin or ROIC is a claim about the layer, not yet about this company.

End with a verdict: **support, mixed, or contradict**, and the two figures that decided it.
