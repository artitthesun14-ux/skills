# Bottleneck analysis

Find the constraint that limits the industry's output, then test whether the company profits from it.

## Find the bottleneck

Check each constraint type against the value chain stages:

- Supply or capacity: not enough units can be made.
- Technology: only a few can make it at the required spec.
- Qualification: customers take years to approve a new supplier.
- Regulatory: permits or licences cap entry.
- Geographic: production is confined to certain places.
- Capital: entry needs more capital than new players can raise.
- Talent: scarce skills limit expansion.
- Manufacturing complexity: yields and know-how take years to learn.
- Lead time: orders wait months or years.
- Supplier concentration: one or two sources for a critical input.

Evidence of a real bottleneck: rising lead times, backlog growth, price increases that stick, allocation of supply to customers, customers prepaying or co-investing in capacity.

## Bottleneck versus investment

A bottleneck creates value for someone; it does not automatically create it for this company. Answer each:

1. **Exposure**: what share of the company's revenue and profit comes from the constrained product?
2. **Pricing power**: has the company raised prices, or are customers capturing the scarcity through long-term fixed contracts?
3. **Capacity**: can the company actually deliver more, or is it constrained too?
4. **Duration**: how long until new capacity, substitutes or design changes relieve the constraint? Name the competitor capacity plans.
5. **Capture**: does the scarcity show up in margins, backlog pricing or returns? If not, say who is capturing it instead.

Conclude: **can the company capture the economics of this bottleneck, and for how long?**
