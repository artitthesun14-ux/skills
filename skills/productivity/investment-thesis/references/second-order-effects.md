# Second-order effects

Trace consequences beyond the obvious one, as far as evidence carries them.

## Layering

```
First order:  AI demand ↑ → GPU utilisation ↑
Second order: → power demand ↑ → grid bottleneck ↑
Third order:  → transformer demand ↑ → lead time ↑ → pricing power ↑ → margin ↑
```

For each layer record the mechanism, the evidence, and its label (FACT / INFERENCE / ASSUMPTION). Aim for two or three layers. Stop at the last layer you can evidence and mark it as the frontier of the analysis.

## Where to look

- **Downstream**: what does the company's growth do to its customers' economics, and will they push back?
- **Upstream**: what does it do to suppliers' pricing and capacity?
- **Competitors**: who expands capacity in response, and when does it arrive?
- **Substitutes**: does higher pricing make an alternative economic?
- **Policy**: does scarcity trigger regulation, subsidies or trade action?

Mark each effect as **helps** or **hurts** the thesis. Effects that hurt feed `counter-thesis.md`.
