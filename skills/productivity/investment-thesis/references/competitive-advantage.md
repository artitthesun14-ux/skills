# Competitive advantage

Identify what the company does that rivals cannot easily copy, and prove it.

## Sources to test

Cost advantage, scale, technology, IP, brand, switching costs, network effects, distribution, installed base, regulation, manufacturing know-how, qualification barriers, ecosystem.

## Four questions per advantage

For every advantage you claim, answer all four:

| Question | What counts as an answer |
| --- | --- |
| **Why** does it exist? | The mechanism: e.g. "customers requalify suppliers only every 5 to 7 years, so incumbency lasts a product cycle." |
| **What is the evidence?** | Observable outcomes: share stability, price premium, retention, win rates, unit cost versus peers, returns versus peers. |
| **How hard is it to replicate?** | Money, time and know-how a rival would need, and whether anyone is trying. |
| **How long can it last?** | Hand to `moat-analysis.md` for the durability horizon. |

## Evidence that separates advantage from luck

An advantage shows up as **outcomes rivals fail to match**: margins above peers through a cycle, share gains without price cuts, customer retention after price increases. Each of these needs its own proof:

- **Growth**: shows demand, which every player may share.
- **High margin**: may be cyclical scarcity; check a full cycle and versus peers.
- **Brand**: proven only by a price premium or retention it causes.
- **Market share**: check its definition and its trend.

Rank the company's advantages from strongest to weakest evidence, and name the single advantage the thesis depends on most.
