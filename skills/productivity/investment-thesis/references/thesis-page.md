# Thesis page

The company page introduces the company first, then tells the thesis as a story, and lets the reader dig for evidence last. Every section starts from its core point and the form its content's shape calls for ([`visual-forms.md`](visual-forms.md)), with words only for what the form cannot carry. A reader new to the company should know what it does and how it earns before the first thesis claim, grasp why the company wins, where the money goes and what survived the debate within a minute more, then open detail only where they care. The page changes how the analysis is shown, never what it says.

## Levels

Detail is disclosed in four levels. The first phone screens of the Company and Thesis tabs hold only Level 1.

1. **Core point** (the first minute): the profile's opening answer, and the thesis hero with its four short cards.
2. **Visual explanation**: the product flow, the revenue tree, the thesis chain and the debate, one line per step, branch, link or exchange, each opening to its detail.
3. **Supporting evidence**: label tags, evidence markers and the cards they open.
4. **Deep dive**: full tables, charts, scorecard, history and sources, behind a disclosure or in the Evidence section.

A reader understands the page from Levels 1 and 2 alone; every detail of Levels 3 and 4 stays one tap away.

## Layout

Seven sections in reading order, each one tab of the company's group in the library (`thesis-library.md`): who the company is, the thesis, the numbers, the price, the debate that tries to break it, and what is left to watch. Each tab has one job, and a fact sits in the tab whose job it serves: a business model in Company, a margin in Financials, a multiple in Valuation. Each tab opens with its question as the heading and a one-sentence answer beneath it, so a reader skimming headings alone gets the company and the thesis. Section numbers refer to `templates/thesis-template.md`.

## Debate UI

The debate reads as a guided conversation between two investors who answer each other, in the page's editorial type: a chat the reader paces, never a live chat, a typing rhythm or a dashboard of cards above it.

- **Room head.** The committee heading, the debate's state in words (concluded), the number of issues and messages, and three controls: Overview (opens What must be true), Show all, and Go to verdict. A legend names the two sides once.
- **Two sides, two shapes.** The Bull speaks from the left in a filled grey bubble, the Bear from the right in a petrol outline (`--cool`, the second side of a pair); each bubble carries a small mark (▲ Bull, ▼ Bear), the name and its message number in the issue, so side never rests on color alone. A bubble is as wide as its text, capped near 600px. Oxblood (`--crit`) marks only a breaker the debate found.
- **One message, one claim.** A bubble shows its attack target as a small label, the claim, and what follows from it. Its evidence folds behind a "show evidence" line that already shows the label tags (FACT, INFER, ASSUME, UNKNOWN) and the number of sources; open, it shows the source line with its evidence markers, and what it proves and does not prove. A turn that makes two points becomes two messages.
- **Flow stepper.** Pinned under the tab bar: Thesis, Bull argument, Bear challenge, Evidence, Rebuttal, Concession, Unresolved, Verdict, each glossed, with a line naming the issue in view. Each message carries the step it performs: Under test is Thesis; a Bull turn before the Bear has spoken is Bull argument; a Bear attack is Bear challenge; a turn in a counter-evidence round, or an evidence conflict, is Evidence; a Bull turn after the Bear, a reply or a reframe, is Rebuttal; a turn that grants the point and the concession box are Concession; the question path and What did we learn are Unresolved. A step is done, here or not yet by scroll position, and disabled where the issue has no such move. Tapping a step reveals and scrolls to its first message.
- **Issues.** Each exchange is one issue: a chip row switches between them, one issue on screen at a time; Show all lays every issue out in full. The issue head shows its number, the lens, the core question and the status in words, with a "summarize this issue" toggle that collapses the conversation to one line per side and What did we learn.
- **Read on.** An issue opens on Under test and its first round; a "read on" control names the next round and reveals it, so the reader paces the argument. Each round opens with a centred indicator ("Round 02 · The counter-evidence") and its question; the key question sits between rounds as the hinge; thesis impact, breakers found and evidence conflicts sit in the conversation where they arose.
- **Concession** shows what changed as Before → After in Strengthened, Unchanged, Weakened or Unresolved, never as points, and the turn that grants it keeps the side's position move (Maintain, Withdraw, Reframe) in words. Show a concession only where the debate recorded one.
- **What did we learn** closes each issue as three rows, Bull proved, Bear proved, Unresolved, then a link to the next issue or to the verdict.
- **What must be true** sits behind the Overview fold, its summary counting where the debate left the items; each item links to the issues that attacked it.
- **The verdict** is the one framed box of the tab: thesis verdict in large type, confidence beside it, at most three reasons beneath. Under the reasons, the page gathers each issue's What did we learn by side (what the Bull proved, what risk the Bear showed, what is still disputed) and lists the breakers with their KPIs as the evidence that would settle the debate. These come from the issues on the page, never new text.
- **Works without script.** Without script every issue and message shows in order; only the stepper, chips and read-on need it.

## Rules

- **Same substance.** Every claim, figure, label, verdict and source of the analysis appears on the page; a fact moves to a deeper level, never off the page. Visual levels (a difficulty meter, a horizon bar, a status mark) come from the analysis's own ratings, and the page says so. Where a visual needs a value the analysis lacks, show UNKNOWN in that slot. If the analysis contradicts itself, show both figures and flag the conflict as UNKNOWN.
- **Labels on key claims.** FACT, INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION and UNKNOWN tags go on the claims a conclusion rests on, not on every sentence.
- **Glossed terms.** When the page is in a language other than English, a term left in English (switching cost, ROIC, royalty, Concession) carries a short gloss in the page's language in parentheses the first time it appears in each section and each exchange, since exchanges open one at a time; every English label gets one wherever it appears. Company and product names, evidence-marker names and the four label tags stay unglossed (the legend covers the tags). A gloss says what the term means here in a few words, and comes from the analysis or a plain definition; where the analysis never defines a term, gloss its role on the page.
- **Evidence markers.** A compact bracketed marker named by source type (Company Filing, Industry Data, Management, Research, Media) opens its evidence card in place. Each card states source (linked), date, claim and why it matters to the thesis. Write a card only when the analysis names the claim's source; other claims keep their label tag alone.
- **Mobile first.** The host's sticky tab bar is the navigation: it marks the tab in view and keeps all seven tabs reachable on a phone. Sections stack in one column; the value chain and wide tables scroll sideways inside their own containers; cards expand in place.
- **Black and white.** Ink and greys only, oxblood for what is critical or contradicts the thesis, petrol for the second side of a pair (`thesis-library.md`, The company page).
- **Form follows content.** Each visual answers its tab's question in the form [`visual-forms.md`](visual-forms.md) picks for its content's shape, and passes that form's fit test.
- **Interaction teaches.** Tapping a bottleneck toggle lights the bottleneck on the map; tapping a breaker found in the debate opens the KPI that monitors it; tapping an item of What must be true opens the exchange that attacked it; tapping a durability row opens that advantage; tapping an evidence marker opens its card.

## Done when

- The Company tab lets a reader with no finance background say what the company does, how the product works, who pays and how it earns; it holds no margin, multiple or thesis claim.
- The thesis hero and 30-second cards fit roughly the first two phone screens of the Thesis tab.
- Every section of the thesis template is placed per the layout table, every figure of the analysis appears on the page, and no fact is repeated across tabs.
- No tab opens with a wall of text: each opens with its question, its answer, then a visual.
- Every visual fits its content's shape per `visual-forms.md`, and no sentence repeats what the visual beside it already shows.
- Every issue of the debate opens from its chip and reads to its end with read on, every step of the stepper lands on a message of that step, every breaker reaches its KPI card, and the verdict is reachable from the room head.
- Every issue shows its core question, Under test and status; every round its indicator; every message its side mark, number and attack target; and the debate tab reads no longer than the debate needs.
- On a page not in English, every English term and label has its gloss per Glossed terms, and the gloss stays secondary to the text it explains.
- At 375px wide the page body has no horizontal scroll.
- Every evidence marker opens a card, every breaker reaches its monitor or says none exists, and the value chain toggles light their stages.
