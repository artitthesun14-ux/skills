# Company profile

The profile introduces the company to a reader who has never heard of it, before any thesis. After reading it, the reader can say in their own words what the company does, how its product works, who pays and how it earns, without knowing finance or investing.

The profile describes; it never argues. Why the business is attractive belongs to the thesis, figures to the financials, the price to valuation. Name a revenue model here, never its margins.

## Six parts, in order

1. **Who is this company?** Two or three plain sentences: the business, the main product or service, and the customer's problem it solves. Open with what the reader can picture, not a slogan or an acronym.
2. **How does the product work?** One sequence: the customer's problem → where the product steps in → how it works → what the customer gets. Write it from this business's real mechanism: a chip-design tool, a cloud database and a CPU each get their own sequence. For a technology business, draw it (an animated or tappable flow, or a diagram).
3. **Who are the customers?** Customer groups as categories. Each says who they are, what they use the product for, why they need it, and who pays versus who uses it when those differ. Name a real customer only when a source shows the relationship; being in the same industry is no evidence of one.
4. **How does it earn?** The business model without financial statements: what is sold (product, license, subscription, service), how it is billed (once, yearly, by usage, other), which kind of product or service brings most revenue, and what brings customers back or renews them where a source says so. When the model has more than one stream and each sits under one segment, draw it as a tree from segment to stream, each leaf saying what is sold and how it is billed; a stream that spans segments keeps the streams as cards. Keep the model's facts apart from any inference about profitability.
5. **Where does it sit in the industry?** A simple value chain: what the company receives and from whom, what it does, whom it delivers to, and why its step matters to the whole. Draw it only when the picture explains the relationships better than a sentence, and claim no more strategic weight than the evidence carries.
6. **Company snapshot.** Four short lines a reader finishes in seconds: What it does, What it sells, Who pays, How it earns.

## Writing

- Overview first, then detail. A concrete example beats an abstract description.
- Explain a technical term the first time it appears, or replace it.
- Label the claims the profile rests on (FACT, INFERENCE, UNKNOWN) and source each key fact. Where no source confirms a part, write UNKNOWN and what would settle it.
- Write as a guide, not a promoter: no adjectives the evidence does not earn.
