# Moat analysis

Judge how long each advantage from `competitive-advantage.md` can last.

## Durability horizons

- **Short**: 0 to 3 years. Scarcity, a product lead, or a capacity gap that rivals are already closing.
- **Medium**: 3 to 7 years. A lead protected by qualification cycles, know-how or a cost gap that takes years to close.
- **Long**: 7 to 15+ years. Structural: installed base with high switching costs, network effects, regulatory licences, scale no rival can economically match.

## Moat anatomy

Write each moat as this chain:

```
Moat
→ Why it exists (the mechanism)
→ What protects it (barriers rivals face)
→ What strengthens it (reinvestment, scale, data, installed base growth)
→ What weakens it (competitor capex, standards changes, customer consolidation)
→ What can destroy it (substitute technology, regulation, customer insourcing)
```

## Signals

- **Widening**: share gains, rising returns on incremental capital, lengthening customer contracts, rivals exiting.
- **Narrowing**: price cuts to hold share, rising customer concentration, new well-funded entrants, falling incremental ROIC.

Give each moat a horizon, the evidence for it, and the leading signal that would move it to a shorter horizon. That signal also goes to the thesis tracker.
